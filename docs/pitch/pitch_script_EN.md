*[Slide: astronaut, alone, window, deep space]*

"Picture a crew member on a Mars mission. Eight months in.

Something feels wrong — not in their body. In their head.

They reach for help. The signal takes 22 minutes to get to Earth.
The answer takes 22 more to come back.

*[Slide: 44 MIN — NO REAL-TIME SUPPORT]*

By then, the moment has passed.

This is the reality of every deep space mission.
NASA's mental health program — the BHP — runs on real-time calls with Earth.
At 44 minutes of delay, that's physically impossible.

NASA knows it. In 2023, they publicly asked the world to build
autonomous behavioral health monitoring systems for astronauts.
Their exact words: these systems do not exist.

*[Slide: AstroKatu]*

AstroKatu is that system.

It runs locally — no Earth connection required.
It monitors passively: voice, sleep, language patterns —
no effort from the astronaut at their most vulnerable moment.

And unlike anything that exists today,
it doesn't measure you against an average astronaut.
It learns *you* — recalibrating what normal looks like
as your body and mind adapt to deep space.

*[Slide: Mars 2035 · Artemis flying]*

The Mars window opens in 2035.
For the first time, we'll send people somewhere we can't bring them back from.

They need a system that goes with them.

AstroKatu. From Tupi — well-being.
Team Pindorama."
